For my BSc thesis, I was part of a four-member research team focused on predicting software defects using object-oriented metrics and machine learning techniques. Within this group project, my individual contribution was primarily centered on methodological design and empirical evaluation.
I was responsible for conducting a comparative analysis of multiple machine learning classifiers, including Logistic Regression, Support Vector Machine, K-Nearest Neighbor, Decision Tree, and Naïve Bayes, to determine their effectiveness in identifying defect-prone modules. This involved applying cross-validation techniques, evaluating model accuracy, and systematically comparing results across different metric groups.
In addition, I contributed to feature selection by analyzing object-oriented metrics and applying statistical methods such as Pearson Correlation to identify the most significant predictors. Through this process, I developed practical experience in data-driven experimentation, performance evaluation, and interpreting model outcomes in a research context.
